## What it is

The abstract of a talk on the MESCAN precipitation analysis in the new
production of CERRA and CERRA-Land under a new C3S contract: forward to near
real time and backward to 1960.

## Numbers we use

- **Continuation:** the original CERRA and CERRA-Land end in June 2021; under
  the new contract production has resumed and reached near real time, and the
  back extension 9/1960–8/1984 is under way.
- **Gauges:** about 8,000 surface observations a day for the present period,
  about 6,500 at the start of the back extension; sources ECA&D, MARS and
  national databases.
- The authors write that the availability of observations changes in time and
  space, which can affect long-term studies, and that they aim to document the
  changes and keep consistency where possible.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3b (#82): the only source found so far on the production after June 2021,
which is half of the CERRA-Land months we use (7/2021–12/2025). It confirms
that the input gauges of the continuation are not the same set as before,
without saying how they differ. The "about 8,000 a day" is above the about
6,500–6,800 a day of 2008–2019 in the original production (Le Moigne et al.,
2021, figure 1.2.2.2), but the two counts may not be made the same way (before
or after quality control), so this is a question, not a result.
