# Comparison of high-resolution climate reanalysis datasets for hydro-climatic impact studies

- **Authors, year, venue:** R. R. Wood, J. Janzing, A. van Hamel, J. Götte, D. L. Schumacher, M. I. Brunner, 2025; Hydrology and Earth System Sciences 29, 4153–4178.
- **Link:** <https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-29-4153-2025>
- **Access:** open, CC BY 4.0
- **Read:** full text, sections on the datasets and on trends (5.2)

## What it is

An evaluation of ERA5, ERA5-Land, CERRA and CHELSA over Switzerland against
gridded observations, by catchment: climatologies, variability, trends
(1986–2020) and the droughts and floods of 1999–2018. Its "CERRA" is CERRA
temperature with CERRA-Land precipitation (section 2.2.2).

## Numbers we use

- **Precipitation:** CERRA-Land is clearly better than the other datasets in
  mean and extreme precipitation; ERA5 and ERA5-Land have problems with
  snowfall and the number of wet days (abstract).
- **Trends of annual mean precipitation, 1986–2020** (section 5.2, figure 7):
  observations show a decrease, significant in about 20% of the catchments;
  ERA5, ERA5-Land and CHELSA show no significant trend anywhere; CERRA-Land
  finds about half of the observed significant decreases, but also many
  significant trends where observations have none, with a wider spread of
  trend sizes than observations.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3b (#82): an independent sign that the long-term behaviour of CERRA-Land
precipitation differs from that of ERA5-Land, in both directions: closer to
the observed trends, but with more trends that the observations do not show.
This is why a comparison of ERA5-Land and CERRA-Land over Belgium needs the
literature beside it: a drift in the ratio could come from either.

## Limits

- Switzerland, complex terrain, against gridded observations that may share
  gauges with CERRA-Land; 1986–2020 only, before the continuation production.
- No test for breaks at a date; trends only.
