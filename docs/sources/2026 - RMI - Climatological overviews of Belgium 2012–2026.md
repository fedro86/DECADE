- **Authors, year, venue:** Royal Meteorological Institute of Belgium (RMI / KMI / IRM), climatological overviews published on meteo.be: yearly overviews for 2018–2025 (PDF, issued between 28 January 2019 and 1 January 2026), monthly overviews for May–September 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 (PDF), summer overviews 2023–2026 (PDF), and the yearly overview web pages for 2012–2017. No individual authors are named.
- **Link:** yearly PDFs `https://cdn.meteo.be/resources/climatology/pdf/klimatologisch_jaaroverzicht_YYYY.pdf`; monthly PDFs `https://www.meteo.be/resources/climatology/pdf/klimatologisch_maandoverzicht_YYYYMM.pdf`; summer PDFs `https://www.meteo.be/resources/climatology/pdf/klimatologisch_seizoenoverzicht_YYYY_S3.pdf`; web pages listed in the table below. - **Access:** open, free download; no licence stated. Dutch versions used. French yearly versions (`bilan_climatologique_annuel_YYYY.pdf`) exist only for 2018–2020; no yearly PDF exists for 2012–2017 (404 on 11/11/2026), so those years come from the web pages, accessed 11/11/2026.
- **Read:** full text of the Uccle and Belgium precipitation parts of every document in the table: the narrative on pages 1–4, the Uccle tables, and the caption lines of the daily-precipitation figures. Monthly bar charts and maps were not read as numbers. The 2025 monthly overviews used here are the reissued versions dated 17 April and 20 May 2026.

## What it is

The RMI's routine climate bulletins for Belgium. Each one gives the values measured at Uccle (Brussels), the RMI's reference station, against the 30-year normal and the records, and adds a short description of the Belgian climatological network: the range of station totals and the regional percentage of normal. Precipitation at Uccle is the manual gauge read at 08 h, the instrument the RMI uses for totals of 24 h and more (Klimaatrapport 2020, p. 12). From the 2021 overview onwards the normals are 1991–2020; before that they are 1981–2010. Records are given for both "since 1991" (or 1981) and the full series since 1833 (precipitation days since 1833; some decade and sub-month records since 1892).

### Ranks, records and Belgian context for the months that matter

- **Before the change (2023 – mid 2024):** July 2023 had 21 precipitation days, a record for 1991–2023, and 131.1 mm, 3rd highest since 1991 (monthly 2023-07, p. 1). Between 16 May and 16 June 2023 no rain fell at Uccle for 32 days, the 2nd longest dry spell since 1892 (monthly 2023-06, p. 1–3). May 2024 had 23 precipitation days, a record for 1991–2024, and 124.9 mm, 2nd wettest May since 1991 (monthly 2024-05, p. 1–2). Every month from October 2023 to September 2024 was wetter than normal at Uccle: 12 months in a row, against a previous record of 8 (March–October 1905) since 1833 (overview 2024, p. 4). Winter + spring 2024 (595.9 mm) and the 12 months ending August 2024 (1203.4 mm) set absolute records since 1833 (overview 2024, p. 5; summer overview 2024).
- **July 2024, the month of the change:** 97.2 mm on 11 days (normal 76.9 mm, 14.3 days). The first 12 days gave 87.0 mm (normal 33.5 mm), a record for those days since 1991, mostly on two days: 41.8 mm on 9 July and 37.0 mm on 12 July; the rest of the month gave 10.2 mm on 6 days (monthly 2024-07, p. 1–2). Over Belgium the month ranged from about 90 % of normal (Gileppe and Warche) to about 135 % (Tournai area) (p. 2).
- **August–September 2024:** August 152.9 mm (1–14 August 109.7 mm; not in the top 5), lowest regional value about 35 % of normal at the coast, highest about 105 % in Belgian Lorraine (monthly 2024-08, p. 1–2). September 123.8 mm, 3rd wettest since 1991; regional values about 115 % to 190 % of normal; 18 thunderstorm days in Belgium, a record since 1928 (monthly 2024-09, p. 1–2). Summer 2024: 323.8 mm, 5th highest since 1991 (record 410.7 mm in 2021) (summer overview 2024).
- **2024 over Belgium:** wetter than normal everywhere but without records outside Uccle; station totals from 816.6 mm (Wevelgem) to 1863.5 mm (Mont-Rigi). Uccle had 11 days with at least 20 mm (normal 5.1); the RMI explains the record by these heavy-rain days, not by the number of wet days (overview 2024, p. 4–5).
- **After the change (2025):** spring 2025 had 54.4 mm (normal 165.6 mm), by far the lowest since 1991 and 2nd lowest since 1833; 1 March – 31 August 2025 had 184.4 mm (normal 399.8 mm), 2nd driest since 1892 (overview 2025, p. 4). Summer 2025: 130.0 mm on 35 days, 2nd driest since 1991 after 2022 (110.6 mm) (summer overview 2025). August 2025: 17.8 mm, 2nd lowest since 1991; most Belgian regions had 25–50 % of normal (monthly 2025-08, p. 1–2). Over Belgium 2025 was drier than normal everywhere, station totals 506.1 mm (Stabroek) to 1245.5 mm (Mont-Rigi) (overview 2025, p. 5). January 2025 still equalled the January record (153.8 mm) and winter 2024–2025 was the 8th wetter-than-normal season in a row (overview 2025, p. 4).
- **2026:** May 79.0 mm, June 114.6 mm (5th highest since 1991), July 10.6 mm on 3 days (2nd lowest since 1991; days a record low since 1991; most of Belgium 1–9 % of normal), August 75.0 mm (3.3 mm on 1–16 August), September 18.4 mm (5th lowest since 1991) (monthly 2026-05 to 2026-09, p. 1–3). Summer 2026: 200.2 mm (summer overview 2026).
- **Other Belgian ranges per year:** 2014: 588.6–1417.0 mm (web page 2014); 2021: 773.5–1574.1 mm; 2022: 523.8–1102.9 mm; 2023: 864.8–1742.9 mm (overviews, text p. 3–4).

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20): is a change in level of the precipitation series around July 2024 climate or an artefact of the ODYSSEY → CIRRUS change?

- **The change falls on a documented climate swing.** The gauge record at Uccle shows the wettest year since 1833 (2024, 140 % of normal) right before and around the change, after a record run of 12 wetter-than-normal months (October 2023 – September 2024), followed by the driest year since 1991 (2025, 74 %), with summer 2025 at 56 % of normal (ratios computed here). Both extremes covered all of Belgium (overviews 2024 and 2025). A radar series that drops from ODYSSEY (2023 – mid 2024) to CIRRUS (2025) is therefore expected to drop in absolute level for real, and such a drop alone is not evidence of an artefact. These documents are the source the project rule requires for that statement, but they cover Belgium only.
- **The climate swing does not explain a step in the ratio to a bridge.** The swing is a change in the weather that ERA5-Land also sees. A month-by-month comparison of each composite with the bridge removes it; a step in the radar/bridge ratio at July 2024 is not explained by these overviews. The same months should be compared on both sides (for example JJA 2023 vs JJA 2025, or the wet 2024 against a wet ODYSSEY year such as 2021 or 2023), because a ratio can still depend on the weather regime (more convective heavy-rain days in 2024, 11 days ≥ 20 mm at Uccle).
- **The overlap days were nearly dry at Uccle.** Of the 87.0 mm that fell on 1–12 July 2024, 41.8 mm fell on 9 July and 37.0 mm on 12 July, so the other ten days, 1–5 July included, had at most 8.2 mm together (computed here from monthly 2024-07, p. 1–2). A direct ODYSSEY–CIRRUS comparison on 1–5 July rests on very little rain, at least at Uccle; this supports using a bridge rather than the overlap alone.
- **Months to watch.** May 2024 (record wet-day count), July 2023 (record wet-day count), the dry spring–summer 2025 and July 2026 (3 wet days) are extremes on both sides of the change; a wet-frequency or level comparison should not be built on these months alone.

## Limits

- **One station.** All monthly and annual numbers are for Uccle; Belgium appears only as a range of station totals and regional percentages read off maps by the RMI. Nothing here covers the Netherlands, western Germany or northern France.
- **Bulletins, not studies.** No trends are computed; ranks and "records" depend on the period (since 1991, since 1892 or since 1833) and are stated as the RMI gives them. For trends, see the Klimaatrapport 2020 summary.
- **Provisional values change.** Values are revised between issues: the 2018 total is 648.9 mm in the 2018 overview, 651.1 mm in the 2022 overview and 650.2 mm in the 2025 overview; the 2018 precipitation days are 146, 141 and 142. The 1991–2020 annual normal appears as 837.1 and 837.3 mm. The 2025 monthly overviews were reissued in April–May 2026. Differences of a few tenths of a mm should not be read as information.
- **Definitions differ.** The 2012–2014 pages count days with ≥ 0.1 mm; the Klimaatrapport 2020 trend analysis uses ≥ 1 mm. The newer overviews do not restate the threshold.
- **Gauge day is 08–08 h.** The Uccle manual gauge is read at 08 h (Klimaatrapport 2020, p. 12), so the daily values do not align with UTC days of the radar.
- **2012–2017 from web pages.** These years were read from the HTML pages (accessed 11/11/2026); no PDF exists, and the pages give the table but no rank since 1833.
