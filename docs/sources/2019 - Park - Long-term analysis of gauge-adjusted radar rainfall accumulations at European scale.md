# Long-term analysis of gauge-adjusted radar rainfall accumulations at European scale

- **Authors, year, venue:** S. Park, M. Berenguer, D. Sempere-Torres, 2019,
  *Journal of Hydrology*
- **Link:** [10.1016/j.jhydrol.2019.03.093](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2019.03.093);
  accepted version on [arXiv:1904.02788](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02788)
- **Access:** journal version paywalled; arXiv version open (revised
  manuscript of 25 March 2019)
- **Read:** full text of the arXiv version

## What it is

ODYSSEY rain-rate composites against SYNOP gauges over Europe, warm seasons
(May–October) 2015–2018, with and without a simple spatially varying gauge
adjustment, for flash-flood hazard assessment.

## Numbers we use

- **Satellite cloud mask since late 2015** (section 1, page 3), citing
  Saltikoff et al. (2018).
- **Median radar/gauge ratio of daily totals, unadjusted** (Table 1; section 4,
  pages 10 and 14–15): 0.48 (2015), 0.50 (2016), 0.61 (2017), 0.89 (2018). The
  authors do not know why it rose so much; they suggest changes in the
  OPERA processing such as the "best possible data" collected from 11 members
  by May 2018 (page 15), and national radar upgrades through 2017 (page 11).
- **After the 2018 change** the gauge-adjusted product slightly overestimated
  from May to July 2018 while the adjustment caught up (page 15).

## Relevance to DECADE

PR #96: an independent, Europe-wide sign that the ODYSSEY rain level rose step
by step in 2017 and strongly in 2018 (warm seasons), in step with the input
change (best data from the members) rather than with a new central filter.
This is consistent with input changes behind our steps after 3/2017; it
does not date a change to 8/2018 specifically.

## Limits

Whole-Europe statistics on SYNOP gauges, rain-rate product, warm seasons only;
no Belgian breakdown; the causes of the level change are the authors'
suggestions, not documented.
