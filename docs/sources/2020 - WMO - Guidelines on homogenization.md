## What it is

The WMO guidance on homogenising instrumental station series, an update of WMO/TD No. 1186 (2003). It covers why series become inhomogeneous, how to build relative (candidate minus or divided by reference) series, how to detect breaks at unknown and documented dates, how to treat monthly data (annual, parallel per calendar month, serial), how to adjust, and which software packages exist; chapter 5 gives the statistical model behind it.

## Numbers we use

- **Difference or ratio, and the log:** section 2.4.5 (p. 19). References are used as candidate minus reference for normally distributed variables and candidate divided by reference for approximately log-normal ones; monthly precipitation is named as a multiplicative, approximately log-normal process. Section 5.1, Remark 1 (p. 41): for quasi log-normal variables such as precipitation the multiplicative model can be made additive with a logarithmic transformation.
- **Known date: the regular t-test:** section 3.2.1 (p. 33). For a normally distributed, uncorrelated candidate-minus-reference series with one break at a known date, the appropriate test is a simple t-test of the difference in means before and after the break; with one break at an unknown date, multiple testing must be accounted for and the appropriate tests are SNHT (Alexandersson, 1986) or the penalised maximal t test (Wang et al., 2007).
- **Documented versus undocumented breaks:** section 2.3.1 (p. 14). Documented change points are tested with the regular Student t test when a reference series is used, and with the regular F test without a reference or when a trend difference is suspected. The critical values of the regular t and F tests are much smaller than those of the maximal tests used for unknown dates; using the regular tests to search for unknown dates gives too many false alarms. Even a documented change needs a statistical test to show that it differs from zero (section 2.3, p. 13).
- **Only well-documented causes:** section 2.3.1 (p. 14). The documented-break test should be used only for a few clearly documented, highly likely causes (relocations, screen changes), not for routine events such as maintenance or calibration; each extra documented break shortens the segments and increases uncertainty.
- **Significance level:** section 2.3 (p. 13). The level is usually 5 %.
- **Serial monthly series: seasonality and autocorrelation:** section 2.2.2 (p. 12). When monthly data are tested as one series, the variability itself can have a seasonal cycle, and the autocorrelation of consecutive months is usually higher than in annual series, non-negligible, and "should be taken into account in the statistical test". Section 5.1, Remark 1 (p. 41) lists the same two problems for serial monthly series (annual cycle of means, variances and covariances; autocorrelation of adjacent months) and states that for series tested separately per calendar month, season or year, seasonality and autocorrelation need not be considered, the values being treated as independent, at the cost of a synthesis step.
- **Parallel monthly series are noisier:** section 2.2.2 (p. 12). Testing each calendar month separately detects seasonally varying breaks, but each such series has one value per year and is noisier; a test on parallel monthly data "helps only in case of dense networks". Annual data have the most favourable signal-to-noise ratio for breaks that affect most of the year (p. 12).
- **Detectability is set by the signal-to-noise ratio:** section 2.4.2 (p. 17). For one break the SNR is the size of the break divided by the standard deviation of the series under test. For a single change point in a series of length N = 600, the chance of reasonably accurate detection is about 53 % at Δ/SD = 0.5, about 91 % at 1.0, 99 % at 1.5 and 99.99 % at 2.0 or more (Figure 4, p. 18, from Table 1e of Wang, 2008a). The stated goal is an SNR above one (sections 2.2.1, p. 11, and 2.12, p. 27). Example: a 0.4 °C break in a series with a standard deviation of 1 °C is difficult or impossible to detect (section 2.4, p. 15).
- **Short series:** section 2.4.1 (p. 17). Series of a few years are problematic because detection depends on a significant difference in means before and after the break, so the length of the homogeneous sub-periods matters as well as the size of the break. Breaks in the first or last few years of a series are difficult to detect and quantify (section 2.9, p. 25).
- **Window around the break:** section 2.6 (p. 22). By default the whole homogeneous sub-period on each side is used; for absolute homogenisation it is common to limit the comparison to 10 years before and after the break; data beyond the adjacent homogeneous sub-periods should not be used. Data close to the break can be excluded when the period just before or after is not representative or the date is uncertain.
- **Reanalysis as reference:** section 2.5 (p. 20). Reanalysis can serve as a reference where no other exists, keeping in mind that it has inhomogeneities of its own; large-scale breaks appear in reanalyses when satellite datasets are introduced or changed.
- **Precipitation corrections annual:** section 2.6 (p. 21). In the HOME benchmark, annual corrections were the most accurate for precipitation; per-calendar-month precipitation corrections were too uncertain.
- **Software for documented breaks with autocorrelation:** section 3.1 (p. 30). RHtests implements break tests that account for autocorrelation and for the distance from the series ends, and includes a test for documented breakpoints; it is not meant for fully automatic use.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20), homogeneity of the bridge: step of the monthly radar / ERA5-Land ratio at 7/2024 and 9/2024 (`opera_ds/homog.py`, `step`). This is the main source for the choice of test.

- **Relative series as log-ratio.** Monthly precipitation calls for the ratio (p. 19), made additive by the log (p. 41). `step` already works in log10 of the ratio, so the step and its test are on the additive scale the guidelines assume.
- **Known date: two-sample t-test, not SNHT or Pettitt.** 7/2024 and 9/2024 are fixed in advance from the dated changes of the radar and ERA5 (metadata), so the regular t-test of the mean log-ratio before against after is the test the guidelines name (p. 33, p. 14), with critical values smaller than those of the maximal tests. SNHT and the penalised maximal t test are for an unknown date (p. 33) and would be needed only if we searched the series for the date. Testing both 7/2024 and 9/2024 is two documented-date tests, not a search; the guidelines say nothing on correcting the level for two candidate dates.
- **The t-test assumes uncorrelated, normal values (p. 33).** Our months are consecutive (serial) values: the guidelines say autocorrelation must then be taken into account (p. 12) but do not give the correction; RHtests has such a test for documented breaks (p. 30); the autocorrelation-aware statistics are in Wang (2008a, J. Appl. Meteor. Climatol. 47, 2423–2444, cited by the guidelines), not read here (paywalled).
- **Seasonality.** The serial monthly ratio can have a seasonal cycle in mean and in spread (pp. 12, 41). The guidelines' way out is to compare calendar months separately (no seasonality, no autocorrelation, p. 41), which with our data means comparing each month after the break with the same calendar month before; with one value per month and per year this is noisy (p. 12). A practical consequence, inferred here and not stated in the guidelines: with a serial test, windows of whole years (12 or 24 months) on each side keep the seasonal cycle from biasing the difference of means; 18 months on each side would count six calendar months twice on one side.
- **Detectable step.** The guidelines give power only for N = 600 and an unknown date (p. 17). For a known date and a two-sample t-test with independent months, the step detectable with 80 % power at the 5 % level is about 0.96 standard deviations of the monthly log-ratio with 18 months on each side, 1.20 with 12 and 0.83 with 24 (computed here with the noncentral t distribution; at 50 % power: 0.67, 0.84 and 0.58). Positive autocorrelation makes these values larger. The SNR in the guidelines is the step divided by the series standard deviation (p. 17), the same scale as `step` compared with `sd_before`.
- **Two products, one ratio.** Like a single difference series (section 5.3.3, p. 44), a step in the ratio cannot be attributed to the radar or to ERA5-Land by itself; ERA5 is named as having breaks of its own (p. 20). Attribution needs other evidence, for example the dated changes in the sources on the radar network and ERA5.
- **Not settled:** the autocorrelation correction and its size for monthly log-ratios; how to remove the seasonal cycle with three to four values per calendar month; whether two candidate dates need a stricter level; normality of the monthly log-ratio.

## Limits

- **Written for station networks:** composite references from several neighbours, long records (decades), at least five stations recommended (p. 17); our pair is two gridded products over Belgium for about four years.
- **Power figures are for N = 600 and an unknown date** (Figure 4, p. 18); they do not transfer to 36–46 months with a known date.
- **No formula for autocorrelated or seasonal monthly series:** the guidelines say both must be handled and point to software and papers.
- **PDF from a mirror,** not from the WMO site (see Access); contents match the edition and ISBN cited.
