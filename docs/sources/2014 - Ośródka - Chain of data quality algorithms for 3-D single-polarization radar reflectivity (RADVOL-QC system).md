# Chain of data quality algorithms for 3-D single-polarization radar reflectivity (RADVOL-QC system)

- **Authors, year, venue:** K. Ośródka, J. Szturc, A. Jurczyk, 2014, *Meteorological Applications* 21, 256–270 (online 22/6/2012)
- **Link:** [10.1002/met.1323](https://doi.org/10.1002/met.1323)
- **Access:** paywalled (Wiley); downloaded through the University of Basilicata
- **Read:** full text (15 pages)

## What it is

The paper describes RADVOL-QC, the IMGW quality-control chain for single-polarization 3-D reflectivity volumes of the Polish POLRAD network (8 C-band radars, 1° x 1 km polar gates, 250 km range, 10 elevations). It has two parallel paths: correction algorithms (SPIKE, SPECK, PBB, ATT) and quality characterization algorithms that each give a quality index between 0 and 1 (SYS, BROAD, GC, SPIKE, SPECK, PBB, ATT). The indices are multiplied into one total QI per gate. The method is checked on one month (May 2010) of 1 km PseudoCAPPI accumulations from two radars, Legionowo (interference) and Pastewnik (mountain blockage).

## Numbers we use

Algorithms are listed in chain order (Table 2). Every QI below is between 0 and 1, and 1 means no problem.

- **SYS (technical radar parameters), QI only.** Targets no artefact from our list; it rates the radar hardware and settings. There are 11 static factors (Table 3, Sec. 2.1). Each gives 0.9 if a limit is exceeded (beam width > 1°, pointing error > 0.1°, sensitivity at 1 km worse than −40 dBZ, antenna speed > 15° s⁻¹, last calibration > 180 days ago, X-band, no radome correction, few time or range samples). The factor is 0.5 if there is no clutter map or Doppler filter. QI_SYS is the product (Eq. 4). If one factor is missing it is set to 1. If two or more are missing, QI_SYS and the total QI become "no data". Input: radar metadata only.
- **BROAD (beam broadening with range), QI only.** Targets no artefact from our list; it rates the loss of resolution with distance. It computes the horizontal and vertical size of each gate, LH and LV, from range, elevation, beam width and gate length (Eq. 5, with gate length 0.3 km for a 2 µs pulse). Each size is mapped linearly to a QI (Eq. 6): LH from 1.1 km (QI = 1) to 2.5 km (QI = 0), LV from 1.5 km to 3.2 km. These limits come from an earlier finding that low-level data are fine up to 89 km and poor beyond 195 km. QI_BROAD = QI_LH × QI_LV (Eq. 7). Input: polar geometry (range, elevation) and radar parameters.
- **GC (ground clutter), QI only.** Targets fixed clutter. The correction itself is left to the radar software (Doppler or statistical filter). RADVOL-QC only flags clutter from a terrain model: a gate is clutter when the partial beam blockage (PBB) rises by more than 0.005 (0.5 %) along the beam (Sec. 2.3, Fig. 5). QI_GC = 0.5 at clutter gates, else 1 (Eq. 8). The QI stays low even after the clutter was removed. Input: DEM, radar position and height, polar geometry. No reflectivity data and no time series are used.
- **SPIKE "wide", correction and QI.** Targets interference spikes (RLAN, sun). A gate is a candidate when the reflectivity variance across the beam (±3° in azimuth) is above 1000 and the variance along the beam (±15 km) is below 5 (Eq. 9). The paper prints the first variance for dBZ and the second for linear Z (mm⁶ m⁻³). A whole ray is flagged when more than 45 % of its gates are candidates. The spike gates are then filled by interpolation from the neighbouring rays. Input: one polar PPI (azimuth x range); single elevation.
- **SPIKE "narrow", correction and QI.** Targets interference spikes at most 7° wide. A gate with echo (Z > −32 dBZ, the radar minimum) is a candidate when, on both sides at azimuth offset d, the neighbour has no echo and is more than 10 dB lower, or is itself a candidate (Eq. 10). The test is run for d = 3°, then 2°, then 1°. A ray is flagged when more than 25 % of its gates are candidates. Filling is the same as for wide spikes. Input: one polar PPI.
- **SPIKE "high", correction.** Targets echoes above 20 km altitude, which are treated as non-meteorological and deleted (Sec. 2.4.1). This is the paper's only height-based rule. Input: beam height, so polar geometry and elevation.
- **QI_SPIKE.** It is 0.5 for every gate of a ray flagged as spike, else 1 (Eq. 11). The whole ray is penalised, not only the spike gates.
- **SPECK, correction and QI.** Targets isolated echoes (speckle), plus "reverse specks", which are isolated no-echo gates inside rain. The window is 3 x 3 gates in polar coordinates, and each elevation is processed alone (Eqs. 12–13). A reverse speck is a gate where fewer than t = 3 gates of the window have no echo. It is filled with the mean of the rainy gates in the window. A speck is a gate where fewer than t = 3 gates of the window have echo. It is set to −32 dBZ. Speck removal is run twice. QI_SPECK = 0.9 at a changed gate, else 1 (Eq. 14). Unclear: whether the centre gate counts in the sum (the sum as written includes it) and whether "not achieved" means strictly below 3. Input: one 2-D field, with gates as pixels.
- **PBB (beam blockage), correction and QI.** Targets blocked sectors. The blocked fraction of the beam cross-section is computed from the DEM with the Bech et al. formula (Eq. 15). Beam height uses an effective Earth radius of 8493 km (Eq. 16). If PBB < 0.7, reflectivity is raised by 10·log10(1/(1 − PBB)) dB (Eq. 18). If PBB > 0.7, the value is taken from the next higher elevation, or set to "no data" if there is none. QI_PBB = 1 − PBB up to 0.7 (Eq. 19). For a gate replaced from a higher elevation it is 0.3 × the QI of that elevation, and 0 at the top elevation (Eq. 20). The alternative threshold 0.5 (Fornasiero et al. 2005) is mentioned. Input: DEM, radar position, polar geometry, several elevations.
- **ATT (attenuation in rain), correction and QI.** Not in our ticket list. It is an iterative gate-by-gate correction along each ray for C-band. Two-way specific attenuation is 0.0044·R^1.17 dB km⁻¹ with Marshall-Palmer Z–R (Eq. 22). It is summed to a path-integrated attenuation (PIA, Eqs. 23–25). Caps on the corrections are said to exist but their values are not given. QI_ATT falls linearly with PIA (Eq. 27). The printed limits are a = 10 dB and b = 5.0 dB, which is the reverse of the order the formula needs (a < b); this looks like a typo and the true values are unclear. Input: polar rays from the radar outward.
- **Total QI.** First, each quality indicator is mapped to a 0–1 index, usually by a linear ramp between two thresholds (Eq. 2). The authors considered minimum, additive and multiplicative ways to combine indices. They chose the product, with weights (Eq. 3), because indicators can be added or removed without re-tuning. In practice all weights are 1 and QI is the product of the 7 indices (Eq. 28). A single zero index makes the total zero.
- **Verification metrics** (Sec. 4.4, monthly accumulations). The first is "symmetry", built from differences between pixels placed symmetrically about the image centre (Eq. 29). The second is "smoothness", built from the local equivalent number of looks (squared mean divided by variance) in an 11 x 11 km window, summed and divided by the total sum (Eqs. 30–31). For May 2010 after all corrections, symmetry rose from 9.07 to 10.12 (Legionowo) and from 2.65 to 3.02 (Pastewnik). Smoothness rose from 270 to 324 and from 224 to 250. Symmetry reacts to spikes and blockage; smoothness reacts to attenuation.

## Relevance to DECADE

This paper is the source of the SPECK, SPIKE ("wide", "narrow"), BROAD and ATT details that the 2022 follow-up (Ośródka and Szturc 2022) only names. It informs H2 (#19) as follows.

- **Usable on our 2-D Cartesian composite as written (our assumption).** SPECK uses only a 3 x 3 neighbourhood and an echo / no-echo test, so it can run on the 2 km grid. We would use the composite "undetect" value in place of −32 dBZ. The thresholds (t = 3, two passes) are from the paper. Applying them to 2 km Cartesian pixels instead of 1° x 1 km polar gates is our adaptation. The total-QI logic (linear ramps, product of indices, a zero index gives zero) is also grid-independent.
- **Needs polar geometry; usable only after adaptation (our assumption).** SPIKE wide and narrow work in azimuth and range around one radar. In a composite, a spike is a radial line from a known radar site. One could resample the composite to a pseudo-polar grid around each radar site and then apply the ±3° / ±15 km windows and the 45 % and 25 % ray rules. This is our idea, not the paper's. In a maximum composite, a spike from one radar is kept even where a neighbouring radar sees no echo. So the "neighbour has no echo" test of the narrow-spike rule should still work outside rain areas. This is our expectation and has not been checked.
- **Cannot be reproduced from our data.** The PBB correction and QI, GC flagging, BROAD and the "high" (> 20 km) rule all need the beam height and elevation of each gate, a DEM, and the radar parameters. PBB replacement also needs several elevations. ATT needs ordered gates along each ray. None of these exist in the 2-D maximum composite.
- **On whether QIND is needed.** The paper's QI encodes blockage, clutter, distance and attenuation, which can only be computed from polar volumes and a DEM. Since we cannot recompute them, the only per-pixel source of this information for our data is a quality field produced upstream. That is an argument for keeping QIND, at least for blocked sectors and range effects. OPERA's `QIND` is described in Saltikoff et al. (2019), section 2.2: the minimum of three indices of the central quality control, weighted by distance and beam height, so it combines differently from the product used here.
- **Long series.** No algorithm here uses a time series; GC relies on a DEM, not on clutter statistics. The paper does note that interference returns at the same azimuths and therefore builds up in monthly sums (Sec. 4.4). This supports a long-series check for fixed clutter and persistent spikes in our 2012–2024 archive. That would be our own method, not the paper's.
- **Evaluation.** The smoothness metric (equivalent number of looks in an 11 x 11 km window) can be computed on any 2-D accumulation. It could be used to compare composite accumulations before and after cleaning (our assumption). The symmetry metric assumes one radar at the image centre, so it does not transfer to a multi-radar composite.

## Limits

- Everything is designed for single-radar polar volumes (POLRAD, 1° x 1 km, 10 elevations, C-band Gematronik). No step is shown on a Cartesian or multi-radar composite.
- There is no algorithm for impossible values in the sense of out-of-range data. Only the 20 km height rule removes physically impossible echoes. There is no algorithm for echoes without rain (biological, anaprop, sea clutter): they are listed as non-meteorological in Sec. 2.4 but not treated. NMET appears only in the 2022 paper.
- Clutter is only flagged from a DEM, not removed. Removal is left to the radar's own software.
- Most QI constants (0.5, 0.9, 0.7, SYS factors) come from the authors' experience or earlier papers, not from a calibration shown here.
- The validation is qualitative maps plus two accumulation metrics for one month and two radars. There are no skill scores against gauges and no false-alarm rates for SPIKE or SPECK. The authors state that efficiency drops when a spike overlaps rain (Sec. 4.1).
- Print issues: the variance units in Eq. 9 (dBZ for one, linear Z for the other) and the inverted ATT limits (a = 10 dB, b = 5 dB) are unclear. We should not copy them without checking the 2022 paper or the BALTRAD code.
- The paper says specks matter little for accumulations from one-elevation products (Sec. 4.1, 4.4). In a maximum composite of many radars, specks from every radar are kept, so this may not hold for us (our assumption).
