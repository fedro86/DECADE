# Multi-decadal analysis of past winter temperature, precipitation and snow cover data in the European Alps from reanalyses, climate models and observational datasets

- **Authors, year, venue:** D. Monteiro, S. Morin, 2023; The Cryosphere 17, 3617–3660.
- **Link:** <https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-17-3617-2023>
- **Access:** open, CC BY 4.0
- **Read:** abstract, section 2.3 (datasets: ERA5, ERA5-Land, MESCAN-SURFEX, CERRA-Land), section 3.1.2 (precipitation climatology), section 3.3 (interannual variability and trends, winter precipitation), section 4 (discussion of artefacts) and section 5 (conclusions). The snow sections were skimmed; figures were not read as numbers.

## What it is

A comparison of global and regional reanalyses (ERA5, ERA5-Land, ERA5-Crocus,
CERRA-Land, UERRA MESCAN-SURFEX, MTMSI) and two regional climate simulations
against observational references over the European Alps, 1950–2020, for
winter (November–April) snow, temperature and precipitation: climatology,
year-to-year variability and trends over windows of 10 to 60 years. The
precipitation reference is LAPrec, a gridded dataset built from a fixed set of
long, homogenised stations.

## Numbers we use

- **Winter precipitation trends, ERA5 products** (section 3.3, Figs 12 and 15):
  ERA5, ERA5-Land and ERA5-Crocus agree with LAPrec on the sign and size of the
  trends, for every window length, with a widespread light decrease of less
  than 7 % a decade over 1968–2017.
- **MESCAN-SURFEX and CERRA-Land** (same section and section 5): MESCAN-SURFEX,
  CERRA and MTMSI give trends of the opposite sign to the other datasets for
  the 10-, 20- and 30-year windows centred on 2005. Over 1968–2017
  MESCAN-SURFEX shows patchy trends beyond ±15 % a decade with a strong
  elevation gradient, "more reminiscent of artifacts than a realistic
  pattern" (CERRA-Land starts in 1984 and is not in that map). The
  conclusions list MESCAN-SURFEX and CERRA-Land among the datasets with
  spurious precipitation trends.
- **Cause given** (section 4): the assimilation of a number of observations
  that varies in quantity and quality over time corrects model biases more
  where and when observations are dense, which shifts the distribution and
  creates artificial trends. The authors say ERA5 and MESCAN-SURFEX favour
  agreement with observations over consistency in time.
- **ERA5 is not free of it either** (section 4): its snow-depth and
  snow-cover trends are about twice the observed ones, from assimilated snow
  observations. For precipitation, ERA5 assimilates no gauges, and its trends
  match the reference.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20), which bridge drifts (comment of 16/11/2026 on #96). It is the second
study, after Wood et al. (2025), that finds artificial precipitation trends in
the MESCAN products (MESCAN-SURFEX, CERRA-Land) and not in ERA5 or ERA5-Land,
and it names the mechanism: a gauge input that changes over time. So, in the
literature, the risk of a drift created by the product is on the CERRA-Land
side, for precipitation.

## Limits

- The Alps in winter, with snow undercatch and strong orography; Belgium is
  low-lying and wet all year.
- Trends over decades, mostly before 2017; nothing on 2012–2025 or on the
  CERRA-Land continuation after 6/2021.
- CERRA-Land is evaluated less than MESCAN-SURFEX, its predecessor; the
  statement about CERRA-Land precipitation trends is made in the conclusions
  and in the short-window comparison, not with a map of its own.
