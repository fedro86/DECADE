## What it is

The official user documentation of ERA5 and ERA5-Land: how the data are produced, how the near-real-time versions (ERA5T, ERA5-Land-T) relate to the final ones, which observations are assimilated, in which parallel production experiments the record was made, and the list of known and resolved issues. It is the only source found that covers 2023–2026.

## Numbers we use

- **Fixed system:** ERA5 page, *Introduction* and *The IFS and data assimilation*. ERA5 uses IFS cycle CY41R2 (operational in 2016) throughout, with 4D-Var in 12 h windows (09–21 and 21–09 UTC) and 18 h forecasts from 06 and 18 UTC. The page warns that a reanalysis still contains "spurious changes" from changes in the observing system.
- **Production experiments in 2012–2026:** ERA5 page, *Production experiments*, table for the HRES. The record is spliced from parallel experiments, and "a discontinuity can occur" at each transition. Transitions inside our period: 1 January 2015, 1 March 2019, 1 September 2021, 1 January 2022 and 23 October 2022 at 22 UTC; the last experiment, on the new ATOS computer, is listed as ongoing. There is no transition in 2023–2026, and none in July 2024. The only documented check of a transition (*ERA5 continuity 2009 2010*) concerns 2009/2010 and the upper atmosphere, not precipitation.
- **ERA5T and final ERA5:** ERA5 page, *Data update frequency* and *Data format*. ERA5T appears about 5 days behind real time and is overwritten on the CDS by the final ERA5 about two months after the month. In GRIB, ERA5T has `expver=0005` and ERA5 `expver=0001`; in netCDF the two cannot be told apart unless one request mixes them.
- **July–November 2024, final ERA5 rerun:** ERA5 page, *Data update frequency* and *Resolved issues*; snow page, sections *Summary* to *Successful rerun*. In July 2024 the ECMWF team found unrealistic snow in the Alps in ERA5T, caused by a quality-control problem and by snow-depth observations not representative of the model grid. The quality control was fixed, the non-representative observations were removed, and the final ERA5 was "rerun from July 2024 onwards". As a result, the final ERA5 differs from ERA5T from 1 July to 17 November 2024, mainly in snow depth, 2 m temperature and dew point over the Alps, with small differences "across many fields" worldwide that should stay within the ensemble spread. Precipitation is not named. The forum announcement of 7 October 2024 states "ERA5-Land is not affected".
- **24–29 June 2024, frozen SST:** ERA5 page, *Known issues*. Global sea-surface temperature was held constant from 24 June 22 UTC to 29 June 22 UTC 2024 (late delivery of the OSTIA product). No effect on precipitation is mentioned.
- **11–18 September 2024, frozen sea ice:** ERA5 page, *Known issues*. Sea-ice concentration held constant (OSI SAF delivery error).
- **1–6 January 2025, satellite data lost:** ERA5 page, *Resolved issues*; ERA5-Land page, *Resolved issues*; forum topic of 9–15 January 2025. A technical problem left "a large part of satellite observations" unused at the start of 2025, giving small but systematic differences, mainly in tropical humidity. ERA5T and ERA5-Land-T were rewritten for 12 UTC 1 January to 12 UTC 6 January 2025, and the final products contain the corrected values.
- **2021 and 2022:** ERA5 page. Final ERA5 differs from ERA5T from 1 September to 13 December 2021 (snow observations in central Asia). On the CDS hourly grids, small local differences exist for 1–24 October 2022 because the regridding software (MIR) changed with the move from Cray to ATOS (24–25 October 2022); they are "not meteorologically significant" and absent on the native grid.
- **Altimeter wave heights:** ERA5 page, *Known issues*. Not available to ERA5 from early February 2021 to mid-January 2022 and from mid-October 2023 onwards. These feed the wave model; no effect on precipitation is mentioned.
- **Observations relevant to precipitation:** ERA5 page, *Observations*, Tables 14–15. Satellite radiances listed with sensitivity to precipitation: AMSR-2 (GCOM-W1), AMSR-E (Aqua), GMI (GPM), MHS (NOAA-18/19, Metop-A/B), MWHS2 (FY-3C), SSM/I (DMSP), SSMIS (DMSP-16/17/18), TMI (TRMM). In-situ: "Ground based radar – Radar precipitation composites – Rain rates". No rain gauge, no Aeolus. The tables give no dates and seem not to list recent satellites (for example none of NOAA-20 or Metop-C); the page refers to section 5 of Hersbach et al. (2020) for dates.
- **Precipitation known issue:** ERA5 page, *Known issues*. "Rain bombs": a few times a year, extreme rainfall in small areas, mostly over high orography in Africa. No precipitation issue is listed for Europe or for 2012–2026.
- **CDS data corruption:** corruption page. A few hundred corrupted fields out of 3.1 billion, caused by a disk problem, since corrected; the entries dated 2013–2018 are none of them precipitation, and ERA5-Land is not in the list.
- **ERA5-Land specific:** ERA5-Land page. It is a land-only replay without data assimilation, forced by the final ERA5 (`expver=0001`) after 1978; precipitation is a forcing field, only interpolated to 0.1° (Table 3 and *Introduction*). Since 1 January 2020 the forcing is interpolated with MIR, which may give "a very small discontinuity" on that day at some high-orography or coastal points. Monthly averaged accumulated fields were wrong from September 2022 to February 2024 (the issue is stated for the monthly averaged product only, fixed from March 2024). No precipitation issue is listed for 2023–2026.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20), homogeneity of the ERA5-Land bridge over 2012–2026 and across July 2024. The project downloads hourly ERA5-Land precipitation (`reanalysis-era5-land`) for 2012-01 to 2026-10.

- **No documented production change in July 2024 for ERA5-Land.** IFS cycle, ERA5 production experiment (ongoing since 23 October 2022) and ERA5-Land forcing are unchanged across the ODYSSEY–CIRRUS switch. The rerun from July 2024 changed the snow quality control of the final ERA5; ECMWF reports no precipitation impact and says ERA5-Land is not affected. Nothing more is documented, so this is absence of evidence, not a proof of homogeneity.
- **Events close to the switch to keep in mind.** Frozen global SST for 24–29 June 2024 and frozen sea ice for 11–18 September 2024 are short and their effect on Belgian precipitation is not documented; excluding these days from a level comparison costs little. Since 1–6 January 2025 was rewritten, data downloaded after mid-January 2025 are already the corrected version.
- **Seams in the calibration period.** The documented experiment transitions inside 2012–2026 are 1 January 2015, 1 March 2019, 1 September 2021, 1 January 2022 and 23 October 2022. If the ODYSSEY side of the bridge is calibrated over several years, these dates are candidate break points to test in the ERA5-Land series (for example against gauges); the 2015 seam is the one Soci et al. (2024) report for soil moisture.
- **Recent months are ERA5-Land-T.** The last 2–3 months of a download are preliminary and are overwritten later; in netCDF they cannot be recognised unless the request mixes the two versions. Months still in ERA5-Land-T at download time should be fetched again before the final calibration.
- **What the documentation cannot tell.** It does not list which satellites (with their precipitation-sensitive microwave channels) entered or left ERA5 between 2012 and 2026, nor any European radar input. The documentation points to Hersbach et al. (2020, section 5), which covers the record from 1979 to about 2019 (Soci et al., 2024, p. 4031) and could not be read here; for 2020–2026 no source was found.

## Limits

- **Living pages:** the pages change; statements refer to the versions dated above. Issues are listed when ECMWF notices them, so the list is not a homogeneity test.
- **No quantitative impact on precipitation** is given for any of the events listed; "within the range of uncertainty" refers to the ensemble spread, which the same page calls over-confident.
- **No observation timeline:** Table 14 has no dates and does not list some recent satellites (none of NOAA-20 or Metop-C); it cannot be used to say which instruments started or stopped in 2012–2026.
- **Global scope:** nothing is specific to Belgium or western Europe, apart from the Alps snow issue of 2024.
