# Regional analysis of convective heavy rain events in the German State of Hesse

- **Authors, year, venue:** M. Perschke, B. Schmalz, E. Ruiz Rodriguez, 2023,
  EGUsphere preprint (discussion started 30/8/2023)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-2023-1907
- **Access:** open, CC BY 4.0
- **Read:** full text of the preprint (not peer-reviewed at the time)

## What it is

Identification and classification of convective heavy-rain objects from the
DWD radar climatology RADKLIM (1 km, 5 min, 2001–2021) over Hesse, in the
Central European low mountain range that extends west to the Ardennes. Each
event gets a characteristic duration (15–90 min); extremes are fitted with a
generalised extreme value distribution.

## Numbers we use

- **8.5 mm/h:** rain-rate threshold for convective cells, taken from earlier
  German work (Brendel et al. 2014); 15 mm/h as a stricter variant. With
  Marshall-Palmer (Z = 200 R^1.6) 8.5 mm/h is about 38 dBZ and 15 mm/h about
  42 dBZ.
- **Event size:** in the DWD heavy-rain catalogue CatRaRE, one-hour heavy-rain
  objects cover 9 to about 100 grid cells of 1 km² (95th percentile), mode 9,
  mean 25.
- **Extreme-event thresholds** from DWD warning criteria: 30, 25, 20, 15 mm/h
  for increasing durations (table 3).
- Characteristic event length 15–60 min.

## Relevance to DECADE

Gives an order of magnitude for the area of convective events (tens of km²) when
setting an absolute area threshold in H1 and D3, and a rain-rate route to the
same 40 dBZ class. Hydrological thresholds are input for V5.

## Limits

German national product, summer half-year only; preprint.
