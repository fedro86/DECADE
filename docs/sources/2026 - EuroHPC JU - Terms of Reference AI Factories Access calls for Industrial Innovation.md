## What it is

The rules of the three access modes through which the EuroHPC JU gives companies
time on its AI Factories (Large Scale, Fast Lane and Playground): who can apply,
how proposals are assessed, and what the awardees must do. DECADE applies to the
Playground after the Leonardo call was lost (P1, #17).

## Numbers and rules we use

- **Playground** (section 1.3): for entry-level users and small allocations. It is
  open at any time and assessed on a rolling basis. An allocation lasts one, two
  or three months. It is on a single system, with fixed amounts of GPU hours per
  application, published on the Playground page. One Playground application can
  be awarded per principal investigator at a time. Innovation decides the
  allocation.
- **Fast Lane** (section 1.2): for HPC-ready users, up to 50,000 GPU hours, one,
  two or three months, one system, rolling.
- **Large Scale** (section 1.1): two cut-offs a month, three, six or twelve months,
  one system, with expert evaluation on innovation and impact (sections 3.2,
  3.2.1: scores 0–5 per criterion, threshold 3 each and 6 in total). The terms
  recommend testing the production code on the same AI Factory through the
  Playground first. The proposal must explain why the work cannot run on a
  smaller system, and may use technical data from a Playground allocation.
- **Assessment of Playground and Fast Lane** (sections 3.1, 5.1.1): an
  eligibility check (self-assessment, with an ethics self-assessment) and a
  technical assessment by the AI Factory. Then access is granted first come,
  first served, until the resources of the call run out.
- **Eligibility** (section 2):
  - the organisation is an industry organisation, company, SME or start-up,
    established in a Member State or in a country associated to Horizon 2020,
    Horizon Europe or Digital Europe (this depends on the system);
  - the principal investigator is employed there when the proposal is
    submitted, and for at least 3 months after the end of the allocation;
  - the status is proven with the VAT number and, if any, the PIC;
  - the purpose is civilian and the proposal complies with the AI Act;
  - the proposal is written in English;
  - no award if another EuroHPC access of the same proposal is running.
- **Scope** (section 1.4): research-oriented AI proposals go to the *AI for
  Science and Collaborative EU Projects* call instead.
- **Systems** (footnotes 3 and 4): Horizon 2020 AI Factories are LUMI,
  MareNostrum 5, Leonardo, Vega, MeluXina and Discoverer. JUPITER is under
  Digital Europe.
- **Obligations** (sections 1.4, 4):
  - data and models stay with the user, and SMEs may exploit them commercially;
  - results are published, except confidential parts;
  - EuroHPC JU and the AI Factory are acknowledged, with a fixed sentence that
    names the project ID and the partition;
  - a final report is due within 3 months of the end, and EuroHPC may publish it
    one year after the end;
  - the awardee takes part in EuroHPC dissemination events at least once in the
    two years after the allocation;
  - there are no extensions and no extra resources.
- **Appeal** (section 5.2): within 15 days of a rejection, to
  aif.access@eurohpc-ju.europa.eu.
