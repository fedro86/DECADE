## What it is

The reference paper of ERA5-Land, the land component of the ERA5 reanalysis, produced by ECMWF for the Copernicus Climate Change Service. ERA5-Land is an offline run of the CHTESSEL land surface model at 9 km and hourly, globally, from 1950 onwards. It is forced by ERA5 near-surface fields interpolated from 31 km. The paper describes the production and compares ERA5-Land with ERA5 and ERA-Interim, mostly for 2001–2018, against in situ and satellite references for soil moisture, snow, lake temperature, river discharge, turbulent fluxes and skin temperature. Precipitation is an input, not an output, and it is not evaluated.

## Numbers we use

- **Precipitation is the ERA5 field, interpolated:** section 2.3 (pp. 4352–4353). The forcing consists of the ERA5 lowest-model-level state (level 137, about 10 m: temperature, humidity, wind, surface pressure) and the surface fluxes: downward shortwave and longwave radiation and "liquid and solid total precipitation". All are interpolated from about 31 km to about 9 km by linear interpolation on a triangular mesh. Table A2 (p. 4376) lists total precipitation (m) in the column of ERA5 forcing fields "interpolated to the ERA5-Land spatial resolution", not among the fields generated by ERA5-Land.
- **No correction of precipitation:** section 2.3 (p. 4353). Earlier land reanalyses corrected the precipitation forcing; ERA5-Land does not. The authors give two reasons: the better quality of ERA5 precipitation compared with earlier reanalyses, and avoiding external data that would delay near-real-time production. Only air temperature, humidity and pressure are corrected for the height difference between the two grids, using a daily lapse rate derived from ERA5.
- **No data assimilation in ERA5-Land:** section 2 (p. 4351). ERA5-Land assimilates no observations directly. Observations act only through the ERA5 forcing, which comes from the 4D-Var and simplified extended Kalman filter assimilation of ERA5 (section 2.3, p. 4353).
- **Forcing "hourly and consistent over the entire production period":** section 2.3 (p. 4353). The paper states this, but it does not test the time homogeneity of the precipitation itself.
- **Model versions:** section 2.4 (pp. 4353–4354) and Table 1 (p. 4356). The land model is IFS cycle Cy45r1 (operational in 2018). ERA5 uses IFS Cy41r2 (2016), 31 km, 137 levels, hourly output. Its uncertainty comes from a 10-member ensemble at 63 km. ERA5-Land has no uncertainty estimate of its own; section 7 (p. 4373) says it uses the ERA5 values.
- **Three production streams:** section 2.1 and Fig. 1 (pp. 4351–4352). Stream-1 starts in 2001 and is initialised from a long ERA5 stream with 3 spin-up years. Stream-2 starts in 1981 and is initialised from an ERA5-Land 1 January climatology of 2001–2018 with 3 spin-up years. Stream-3 covers 1950–1980 and is initialised from a 1981–2010 climatology with 1 spin-up year (1949). Discontinuities between streams are possible for slow variables such as deep soil moisture (Fig. 3). Precipitation is taken from ERA5 and has no spin-up. None of the stream boundaries falls in 2012–2026.
- **Observing-system changes:** section 1 (p. 4349). Land data assimilation can create "temporal and spatial inconsistencies" because the availability of observations changes. The offline ERA5-Land design avoids this for the land state. The paper does not discuss how changes in the observations assimilated by ERA5 affect ERA5 precipitation over time.
- **Accumulation convention:** section 5, Eqs. (6)–(8) (p. 4368). Water and energy fluxes are accumulated from the start of the forecast at 00:00 UTC, "with a maximum of 24 h accumulation period". For example, the 12:00 UTC value of a flux holds the amount since 00:00 UTC. The daily amount of a flux is therefore the value at step 24 (Eq. 8), i.e. the 00:00–24:00 UTC day.
- **Grid in the Climate Data Store:** section 5 (p. 4367). The native grid is TCo1279 (section 2, p. 4351). The Climate Data Store serves the data interpolated to a regular 0.1° latitude–longitude grid. The ERA5 forcing is also available, already interpolated to the ERA5-Land grid.
- **Latency:** section 5 (p. 4367) and Table 1. The final product is 2–3 months behind real time. A preliminary product with about 5 days' delay was planned for 2021.
- **Precipitation biases acknowledged:** section 7 (p. 4373). Precipitation is called one of the most important drivers. Systematic precipitation biases of the model can spread into the land states, and ERA5 "still has large precipitation biases, especially in tropical regions". Future versions might use bias-adjusted forcing (WFDE5, 0.5°) or a rescaling to a high-resolution climatology.
- **Snowfall bias in Europe:** section 6 b (p. 4371). Snowfall in the IFS has a systematic positive bias in Europe. In ERA5, snow-depth assimilation partly removes it; ERA5-Land has no such assimilation.
- **Indirect hydrological check:** section 4.4 and Fig. 13 (p. 4365). River discharge from GloFAS improves with ERA5-Land runoff compared with ERA5 runoff: median KGE′ rises from 0.26 to 0.37 and the median bias ratio from 0.73 to 0.89 (1285 stations, 2001–2018). Both runs use the same precipitation, so the gain comes from the land model and the resolution, not from better rainfall.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20), choice of the bridge dataset between ODYSSEY and CIRRUS.

- **Testing ERA5-Land precipitation means testing ERA5 precipitation.** ERA5-Land adds no precipitation physics, no gauge information and no correction; it only interpolates the ERA5 field. Any statement on the level, the wet-hour frequency, the drizzle or the extremes of the bridge must therefore rely on ERA5 precipitation studies (for example Wood et al., 2025, for wet days and extremes). It cannot come from this paper.
- **Effective resolution:** the 0.1° grid carries no precipitation detail finer than ERA5's 31 km, because the field is only interpolated. If the radar is compared with ERA5-Land at 0.1°, the radar field is sharper than the bridge, and its local maxima have no counterpart. This bears on the scale at which the quantiles are compared. The paper does not settle that scale.
- **Homogeneity across July 2024:** by design, nothing in ERA5-Land changes at that date. The land model cycle and the ERA5 system are fixed, ERA5-Land assimilates nothing, and the production streams start in 1950, 1981 and 2001. This is the property a bridge needs. What the paper does not show is the stability of ERA5 precipitation itself under changes in the assimilated observations; that has to come from ERA5 documentation.
- **Accumulation:** the hourly precipitation in ERA5-Land is accumulated from 00:00 UTC. Hourly amounts are obtained by differencing consecutive steps, and the 00:00 UTC value holds the full previous day. A daily comparison with ERA5-Land uses 00–24 UTC days, while CERRA-Land is analysed on 06–06 UTC days. Mixing the two bridges therefore needs the radar to be accumulated over two different daily windows.
- **Sub-daily behaviour:** hourly output exists, but the paper says nothing about the quality of the hourly precipitation or its diurnal cycle.

## Limits

- No precipitation evaluation at all: no wet-hour frequency, intensity distribution, extremes or diurnal cycle. The evaluated variables are land states and fluxes.
- The evaluation period is mostly 2001–2018 and the paper predates the 2024 change from ODYSSEY to CIRRUS. The homogeneity of ERA5 precipitation in 2024 is outside its scope.
- The precipitation biases named in the paper concern the tropics and snowfall in Europe. There is nothing specific to rainfall in Belgium or to summer convection.
- Statements on consistency concern the offline land state (spin-up, streams, no land assimilation). They do not transfer automatically to the precipitation forcing, which inherits whatever inhomogeneity ERA5 has.
