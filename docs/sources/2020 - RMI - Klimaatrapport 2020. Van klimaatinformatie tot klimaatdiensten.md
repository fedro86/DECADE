## What it is

The RMI's five-yearly climate report. Chapter 1 analyses the long series of Saint-Josse-ten-Noode (1833–1885) and Uccle (from 1886) and, for precipitation, answers a set of questions (does it rain more, are heavy rain, extremes, snow and droughts more frequent). Annual and seasonal totals come from the manual gauge read at 08 h; sub-daily extremes from the 10-min pluviograph (1898 onwards) and the automatic gauge that replaced it. Each series is shown with a LOESS curve over the whole period and a linear regression from 1981 to the last year (2019, or 2020 for spring droughts); the slope is printed only when significant at 90 % (box, p. 7).

## Numbers we use

- **Method:** trend from 1981 by linear regression on annual values; "significant" means the slope can be attributed to a real trend with at least 90 % probability (box "Bepaling van klimatologische trends", p. 7). No test name, no correction for autocorrelation, no confidence interval given.
- **Gauge:** the manual gauge read every morning at 08 h, cumulative over 24 h, is used for totals of a day or more; the pluviograph (10 min since 1898) and, for about ten years, an automatic gauge for shorter durations (p. 12).
- **Annual total, Uccle:** 1961–1990 mean 821.1 mm; 1833–2019 mean 793.3 mm (Fig. 1.10, p. 13). First 30 years 768 mm against last 30 years 839 mm, +9 %; 19th century 762 mm, 1901–1980 796 mm (+4 %), 1981–2019 843 mm (+6 %). **Since 1981: slight, not significant increase** (p. 13; the Fig. 1.10 legend reads "trend since 1981 not significant").
- **Seasons since 1981 (p. 13):** winter positive, not significant (the long-term rise from 175 to 230 mm between the first and last 30 years, +31 %, is since 1833); **spring significant decrease of about −9 mm per decade**, which the RMI ascribes to wet springs in the 1980s followed by mostly dry springs since the 1990s; **summer small, not significant increase**; autumn no trend.
- **Precipitation days (≥ 1 mm):** no clear long-term trend and no significant trend since 1981 (p. 13).
- **Heavy-rain days (≥ 20 mm), p. 14:** summer (JJA) significant increase since 1981 of +0.6 days per decade (legend: 0.59 days/decade); the 5 highest summer values are after 2001; mean 1892–2019 1.5 days per summer (Fig. 1.11). Whole year: significant, about +0.5 days per decade. Share of heavy-rain days in the annual total: significant +2 % per decade since 1981 (legend: 2.05 %/decade); mean 12.8 %; highest 37 % in 2004 and 30 % in 2011 (Fig. 1.12).
- **Annual maxima, p. 15:** since 1898 the 24 h and 10-day maxima increase and the 1 h maxima show almost no trend; since 1981 only the 1 h maximum has a significant trend, about +3 mm per decade (mean 1898–2019 18.0 mm, Fig. 1.13); for April–September the 1 h maximum rises by +2.8 mm per decade. 24 h and 10-day maxima: no significant change since 1981.
- **Dry days and droughts, p. 19–20:** annual dry days (< 1 mm): no significant trend; spring dry days +3.1 days per decade since 1981 (legend 3.38); longest spring dry spell significant increase since 1981 (legend 1.48 days/decade, Fig. 1.20); winter longest dry spell −1.3 days per decade; no significant trend for other seasons, the year or April–September.
- **Conclusion (p. 16):** precipitation at Uccle shows much weaker trends than temperature, partly because of its large variability; since 1981 only spring totals (down) and hourly maxima (up) have significant trends.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20): can a level change around July 2024 be climate?

- **A published trend exists for a period ending after 2015, but it does not reach 2024.** The last year analysed is 2019 (2020 for spring droughts). For annual and summer totals at Uccle the 1981–2019 trend is not significant. A step in the level of a precipitation series at July 2024 cannot be explained by a published trend: the only significant trend in seasonal totals, spring −9 mm per decade, amounts to about 1 mm per year, far below any step visible in a few months (computed here).
- **What can change slowly is the tail.** The summer heavy-rain days (+0.6 days per decade) and hourly maxima (+3 mm per decade) increase significantly since 1981. Over the few years around the change these are small; they matter only if the method relies on the extreme tail of a short period.
- **What changes quickly is variability.** The report stresses the large year-to-year variability; the 2024 record and the dry 2025 (see the RMI overviews summary) are of this kind, not a trend. Comparing ODYSSEY and CIRRUS against the bridge month by month removes them.

## Limits

- **One station:** Saint-Josse-ten-Noode / Uccle only for precipitation trends; nothing on the rest of Belgium or the neighbouring countries.
- **Ends in 2019:** 2020–2026, the radar change and the record wet 2024 are outside the analysis.
- **Simple statistics:** linear regression from 1981, 90 % significance, no test name, no uncertainty range; a trend that is "not significant" is not quantified, so its size is unknown.
- **Daily gauge 08–08 h and ≥ 1 mm days:** different from the radar day and from the ≥ 0.1 mm count of some bulletins.
