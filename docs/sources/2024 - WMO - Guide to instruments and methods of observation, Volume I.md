## What it is

The WMO reference guide on meteorological instruments and observing
practice. Chapter 6 covers precipitation gauges (manual, weighing,
tipping-bucket), their siting, errors and corrections.

## Numbers we use

- **Errors of a precipitation gauge** (6.2.1.2), as a list of terms:
  - wind-field deformation above the orifice: typically 2–10% for rain and
    10–50% or more for snow;
  - wetting loss on the collector walls: 2–15% of measured values in summer,
    1–8% in winter, up to 0.2 mm per observation;
  - evaporation, up to more than 0.8 mm a day in late spring for gauges
    without a funnel;
  - in- and out-splashing, 1–2%;
  - random observational and instrumental errors.
- **Tipping-bucket gauges** (6.2.3, 6.2.3.2):
  - without an intensity-dependent correction they underestimate high rain
    rates and overestimate low ones;
  - the water lost while the bucket tips is a systematic mechanical error
    that grows with intensity and becomes appreciable above 100 mm h⁻¹;
  - it is reduced by a dynamic calibration and an intensity-dependent
    correction.
  - Other errors: evaporation from the bucket in light rain, water left on the
    bucket (waxed buckets needed 4% less volume to tip), friction and
    levelling, delay in light rain.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20), the check of the bridges against the RMI automatic stations
(comment of 16/11/2026 on #96, caution 2). It gives the size of the gauge
errors as a reference: a few percent for wind in rain, a loss in heavy rain
that depends on intensity. Most of these errors are constant in time for a
given gauge and site, so they change the level of a bridge / gauge ratio but
not its trend; what can change the trend is a change of gauge or site, or a
change in the share of heavy or windy rain, which is why the trend test is
repeated without the stations with a step and without the wettest days.
