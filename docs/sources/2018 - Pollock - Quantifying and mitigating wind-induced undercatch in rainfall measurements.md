# Quantifying and mitigating wind-induced undercatch in rainfall measurements

- **Access:** open, CC BY (downloaded by hand: automatic download blocked)
- **Read:** full text (13 pages): abstract, key points, sections 1 (background on undercatch), 2–3 (sites, gauges, results, Table 2, regression on wind speed) and 4 (conclusions).

## What it is

A field comparison in northern England of identical tipping-bucket gauges of
different shapes and heights against a gauge in a pit (the reference that
removes the wind effect), at a windy upland site and an exposed lowland site,
to measure the rain lost to the wind (undercatch) and how it grows with wind
speed.

## Numbers we use

- **Conventional cylinder gauge at 0.5 m** (abstract and section 3, Table 2):
  it catches 9.4% less than the pit gauge at the lowland site and 23.8% less
  at the upland site, on average; the largest difference is 38.5%.
- **Improved-aerodynamic gauge at 0.5 m:** 3.4% (lowland) and 11.2%
  (upland).
- **Mounting height:** the same gauge loses 11.2% at 0.5 m and 17.5% at 1.5 m
  (upland).
- **Wind speed:**
  - in 2 h intervals of large frontal events, the undercatch is linear in
    the wind speed at gauge height (81% of the variance explained, 58% with
    1 h);
  - at 6 m s⁻¹ the model predicts 16.7%.
- **Earlier studies** (section 1): 4% between pit and above-ground gauges in
  typical events (Duchon and Essenberg 2001), 2–10% in general (Sieck et al.
  2007).

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20), the check of the bridges against the RMI automatic stations
(comment of 16/11/2026 on #96, caution 2). Wind undercatch is a loss of
several percent up to tens of percent that grows with wind speed, so in windy
or stormy years a gauge catches relatively less. For the trend test this
matters only if the windiness of the rain changes over 2012–2025; the test
without each station's wettest days is the check we can run with the data we
have, since the RMI stations do not publish wind at gauge height with the
precipitation.

## Limits

- Two sites in northern England, mostly frontal rain; the sizes depend on
  site exposure, gauge shape and height, which are not published for the RMI
  stations.
- Rain only; snow is out of scope.
- Event and 1–2 h scales, not monthly sums.
