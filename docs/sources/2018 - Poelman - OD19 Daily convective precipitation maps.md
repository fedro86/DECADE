# OD19 Daily convective precipitation maps

- **Authors, year, venue:** D. Poelman, L. Delobbe (RMI), 1/12/2018,
  EUMETNET OPERA work package OD19 report
- **Link:** https://www.eumetnet.eu/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Daily_convective_precipitation_Maps_OPERA.pdf
- **Access:** open (no licence stated)
- **Read:** full text (13 pages)

## What it is

A study for EUMETNET OPERA on daily maps of convective precipitation over
Europe from the OPERA composites, the EUCLID lightning network and national
hail products (Switzerland, Netherlands, Belgium). 25 days with widespread
convection in 2014–2017, chosen from the lightning data. It uses the OPERA
surface reflectivity/rain-rate composite at 15 min (the report lists three
composites: surface reflectivity/rain rate, maximum reflectivity, hourly
accumulation).

## Numbers we use

- **40 dBZ:** "convective accumulation" counts only rain with reflectivity above
  40 dBZ (section III).
- **40 and 48 dBZ:** daily maximum reflectivity above 40 dBZ, or lightning,
  marks convection; above 40 or 48 dBZ together with lightning marks more severe
  convection (figures 7–8).
- **Hail:** probability of hail from the height difference between the 45 dBZ
  echo top and the freezing level (Waldvogel et al. 1979); areas with
  probability above 60% match lightning well.

## Relevance to DECADE

The closest reference to our data: OPERA composites, pan-European, written by
RMI. Supports 40 dBZ as the convective threshold on OPERA. The authors call the
threshold "a very simple approach" and point to Steiner et al. (1995) and
others for proper convective/stratiform separation.

## Limits

Daily maps, not 5 or 15 min classification; no area or duration threshold;
lightning is not part of our open data.
