# The ERA5 global reanalysis

- **Authors, year, venue:** Hersbach, H., Bell, B., Berrisford, P., Hirahara, S., Horányi, A., Muñoz-Sabater, J., Nicolas, J., Peubey, C., Radu, R., Schepers, D., Simmons, A., Soci, C., et al. (45 authors), 2020. Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society 146, 1999–2049.
- **Link:** [10.1002/qj.3803](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3803)
- **Access:** open access, CC BY (Creative Commons Attribution). The Wiley site blocks automatic download; the PDF was downloaded by hand.
- **Read:** full text of abstract and sections 1–3 (configuration, 4D-Var and VarBC, background error, production streams, timely updates; pp. 1999–2008), section 4.5 (observation handling, pp. 2010–2011), section 5 in full with Tables 2–4 and Figs 3–6 (observing system, pp. 2010–2021), section 7 (uncertainty, departures, increments; pp. 2025–2030), sections 9.2–9.3 with Figs 23–24 (water balance and precipitation, pp. 2032–2035) and section 10 (strengths, known issues, related products; pp. 2037–2039). Fig. 24 is known only from its caption and the text.

## What it is

The reference description of ERA5, the fifth ECMWF atmospheric reanalysis, produced for the Copernicus Climate Change Service with one fixed system, IFS Cy41r2 (operational from 8 March to 21 November 2016), at 31 km and hourly output, with a 10-member ensemble for uncertainty. The paper covers 1979 to about 2019 (comparisons end in August 2019, with ERA-Interim). It documents the model and assimilation changes since ERA-Interim, the observing system instrument by instrument, the production streams, and a basic evaluation, including precipitation against GPCP, GPCC and TRMM/3B43.

## Numbers we use

**System and production**

- **One system, changing observations:** abstract and section 2.1 (pp. 1999, 2002). ERA5 runs Cy41r2 throughout, with 12-h 4D-Var windows (09–21 and 21–09 UTC). The 06 and 18 UTC short forecasts carry information to the next window; all components are coupled in them (p. 2002).
- **VarBC extended to radar–gauge composites:** section 2.2 and Table 2 (pp. 2003, 2005). Variational bias correction, applied to radiances only in ERA-Interim, also covers ground-based radar–gauge composites, total column ozone, aircraft temperature and surface pressure in ERA5. The bias parameters carry over from one cycle to the next with no evolution model; for most satellite data each bias group is weighted as 5,000 observations (p. 2005).
- **Background error by period:** section 2.4 (p. 2007). The climatological part of B uses one estimate for 1979–1999 and another (the Cy41r2 default) from 1 January 2000. The paper says this handles the observing system's evolution "in a rather crude way"; the 2000 switch gives the largest jump of the record (stratospheric temperature, p. 2008). Our period has no change of B_cli.
- **EDA weighting bug, 2010–2014:** section 2.4 (p. 2007). For data produced before March 2017, the weight of the ensemble B was inadvertently kept at 0.15 at all scales. This affects January 2000–June 2005 and **January 2010–October 2014**. According to the paper, the effect on HRES products is minor; it is larger on the ensemble spread (Fig. 12c shows ozone spread raised from the January 2010 seam, p. 2026).
- **Production streams:** section 3 and Table 3 (pp. 2007–2008). HRES streams after 2005 start in Jan 2008, **Jan 2010, Jan 2015 and Mar 2019**; EDA streams in Jan 2010, Jan 2015, Mar 2019. The mid-2010s stream was initialised from the ECMWF operational analysis of 30 May 2014 (Cy40r1) and streams overlap by one year for spin-up. The March 2019 stream is a continuation of its predecessor and also produces the timely updates. The authors report "very few significant jumps" at seams in the troposphere and stratosphere, but discontinuities in the mesosphere and deep soil (p. 2008), and root-zone soil moisture discontinuities at some seams (section 10.2, p. 2039).
- **Preliminary vs final product (ERA5T):** sections 1 and 3 (pp. 2001, 2008), section 10.1 (p. 2038). One day is added daily with a 5-day delay; each month the preliminary month is replaced by a quality-checked final one 2–3 months later. The two are expected to "rarely differ", and users are notified if they do. A quality check against extreme 10 m winds was added to the timely updates from 19 February 2020 only (p. 2039), so the timely stream's checks can differ from those of the back production.
- **Precipitation products:** section 3 (p. 2008). On the CDS, precipitation and other fluxes are hourly series that combine analysis fields with short-range forecasts. The paper does not spell out the accumulation rule (06/18 UTC forecasts, steps 1–12); Table 6 notes that its energy budgets come from the first 12-h forecasts (p. 2022).

**Observations that influence precipitation**

- **Volume:** section 5.1 (p. 2010). About 0.75 million observations per day in 1979 and about 24 million per day by January 2019, from over 200 instruments or conventional types. No rain gauge amounts are assimilated; in-situ inputs are pressure, wind, temperature, humidity, and (in the land analysis) screen-level temperature and humidity, soil moisture and snow depth (pp. 2010, 2013).
- **Ground-based radar–gauge rain rate, USA only:** sections 5.1, 5.3 and 9.3 (pp. 2010, 2013, 2015, 2034). ERA5 assimilates NCEP Stage IV estimates (NEXRAD combined with gauges) "from 2009", or from mid-2009 in section 9.3, as one bias group with a six-parameter bias model. Fig. 3k shows of the order of 10³ rain-rate observations per day (30-day mean), with large daily variability, from 2009 to 2019. In Fig. 4 the radar–gauge bar is ERA5-only until 2016; after the switch of reference to ECMWF operations it shows red segments, that is, periods where operations used Stage IV but ERA5 did not. No other ground-based precipitation input is mentioned, none in Europe.
- **All-sky microwave imagers (rain- and cloud-affected radiances):** section 4.5 and 5.4.3, Table 4, Fig. 5 (pp. 2010, 2014, 2017–2018). Imager radiances are assimilated in all-sky conditions over ice-free ocean, informing lower-tropospheric humidity, cloud liquid water and rain. Dates in the text: SSM/I CM SAF FCDR August 1987–December 2008, TMI from 2005, AMSR-E from 2009, SSMIS F-17 from 2009, AMSR2 from 2012, GMI from 2015; SSMIS humidity channels also from F-18. Table 4 gives TMI 2005–2015. **At most three imagers are used at any one time**, through the blacklist, to avoid bias problems (p. 2018). Read from Fig. 5: AMSR-E ends about late 2011, TMI about early 2015, and a short gap in AMSR2 appears around 2014–2015.
- **All-sky microwave humidity sounders:** section 5.4.2, Table 4, Fig. 5 (pp. 2014, 2017–2018). MHS on NOAA-18, NOAA-19 and MetOp, and MWHS-2 on FY-3C, all-sky; MWHS-1 (FY-3B) and AMSU-B clear-sky only. Table 4: MWHS from 2012, MWHS-2 from 2014. From Fig. 5: MHS MetOp-B from about 2013, MetOp-C from about 2019; MHS NOAA-18 ends about late 2018. SAPHIR is not used in ERA5 (red, about 2017–2019).
- **Microwave temperature sounders:** section 5.4.1, Fig. 5 (pp. 2016–2017). By early 2019, nine instruments (seven AMSU-A, two ATMS). From Fig. 5: ATMS S-NPP from about 2012, NOAA-20 from about 2018; AMSU-A MetOp-B from about 2013, MetOp-C from about 2019. ATMS also provides humidity sounding (p. 2016).
- **Hyperspectral infrared:** section 5.5.2, Table 4, Fig. 5 (pp. 2014, 2017–2018). AIRS from October 2002; by June 2019 two CrIS (S-NPP, NOAA-20) and two IASI (MetOp-A, -B). From Fig. 5: IASI MetOp-B from about 2014, CrIS S-NPP from about 2015, CrIS NOAA-20 from about 2018.
- **Geostationary water-vapour radiances:** section 5.6, Fig. 5 (pp. 2017–2019). Meteosat (-5 to -11) over Europe and Africa since 2001. From Fig. 5: Meteosat-10 from about 2013, Meteosat-11 from about 2018, Himawari-8 from about 2016, GOES-16 from about 2018.
- **GNSS radio occultation:** sections 5.1 and 5.8, Fig. 6 (pp. 2010, 2019–2020). From 2001, large quantities from 2006 (COSMIC); unbiased anchors for VarBC. From Fig. 6: MetOp-B from about 2013, TanDEM-X about 2015, FY-3C about 2018, MetOp-C and KOMPSAT-5 about 2019; the COSMIC-1 satellites thin out from about 2016–2017.
- **Scatterometers:** section 5.9, Table 4, Fig. 6 (pp. 2014–2015, 2019–2020). ASCAT on MetOp-A/-B (from 2007) and Oceansat-2 (2013). Reprocessed ASCAT is used before 2014 and the operational stream from 2014, for wind and soil moisture.
- **Conventional, 1 January 2015:** section 5.3 and Figs 4 and 14 (pp. 2015–2016, 2027). BUFR radiosonde (TEMP) and BUFR land SYNOP reports are used from 1 January 2015, giving a denser vertical sampling. On the same date, radiosonde temperature bias correction switches from RICH to the operational scheme. Fig. 3e shows the number of assimilated specific-humidity observations rising steeply from about 2015 in ERA5. This date coincides with the January 2015 stream seam.
- **Aeolus** is not mentioned anywhere in the paper.

**Precipitation evaluation and consistency**

- **Correlation with GPCP:** abstract and section 9.3 (pp. 1999–2000, 2035). Whole-globe correlation of monthly precipitation for 1979–2018 is 77 % for ERA5 and 67 % for ERA-Interim (GPCP, 2.5°); 63 % and 50 % with GPCC (1°, land).
- **Against TRMM/3B43, 1998–2018:** section 9.3, Fig. 24 (pp. 2034–2035). Mean absolute difference over 50° S–50° N is 0.58 mm day⁻¹ for ERA5, 0.65 for ERA-Interim; correlation of monthly anomalies 70 % and 63 %. The comparison is not independent: both use microwave imagery, and Stage IV uses US gauges that also enter GPCC.
- **Shifts against GPCC/GPCP:** section 9.3 (p. 2035) and Fig. S5. ERA5 shows shifts over time, notably a decline in the differences over a few years centred on 2000 (Congo Basin, south-east China), "steadier thereafter". ERA5 land precipitation declines from the 1980s–1990s to the 2000s, not seen in GPCC or GPCP (section 9.2, p. 2034).
- **Global water balance:** section 9.2, Fig. 23 (pp. 2033–2034). Balance between precipitation and evaporation is good for about twenty years from the mid-1990s. In Fig. 23b, ERA5 global evaporation drops from about 2016 while precipitation stays near 2.95–3.0 mm day⁻¹; in Fig. 23f the land precipitation of ERA5 follows the GPCP and GPCC curves through 2010–2019, with no visible step (values read from the figure).
- **Drying from microwave imagers:** section 7.4, Fig. 16 (p. 2029). Mean analysis increments show a drying tendency at 850 hPa over the oceans from 1988 "to the present day", coinciding with imager assimilation; the mechanism is unclear.
- **"Rain bombs":** section 10.2 (p. 2038). ERA5 occasionally gives unrealistically high precipitation at isolated grid points (about ten episodes a year, mostly in Africa, in orography), a feature of the convection scheme since Cy40r1. Since ERA5 uses Cy41r2 throughout, this is a property of the whole record, not a dated change.
- **Window seams:** section 10.2 (p. 2038). Analysis increments can cause systematic jumps between 12-h windows; the example is a 10 m wind jump of about 0.25 m s⁻¹ over Paris at 10 UTC.
- **Temporal consistency in general:** section 10.4 (p. 2039). The authors name the interaction between model bias and an evolving, biased observing system as a concern for temporal consistency. They also say that observation errors are kept constant over time in ERA5.
- **ERA5-Land:** section 10.3 (p. 2039). ERA5-Land (9 km) is one single simulation forced by ERA5 near-surface fields, which makes soil parameters more homogeneous across ERA5 streams. The paper says nothing about its precipitation, which comes straight from ERA5.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20), homogeneity of the ERA5-Land bridge over 2012–2026 and across July 2024.

- **What it covers:** the observing system and production of ERA5 up to about mid-2019. For 2012–2019 the paper gives the dated list of new and ending instruments above. Most changes add satellites gradually; the inputs most directly tied to precipitation (all-sky imagers and humidity sounders) change by single instruments (AMSR-E off about 2011, AMSR2 2012, TMI off and GMI on 2015, MHS MetOp-B about 2013, MetOp-C about 2019). The cap of three imagers at a time limits how much the imager count can vary. The only ground-based precipitation input is Stage IV over the USA from 2009, so there is no European radar or gauge precipitation input whose start or stop could create a step over Belgium.
- **Coinciding dates to keep in mind:** 1 January 2015 combines a stream seam, the switch to BUFR radiosonde and SYNOP reports, and a new radiosonde bias-correction scheme. January 2010 to October 2014 has a different EDA weighting from later data, said to be minor for the HRES. These are the candidate dates for a step inside 2012–2019; the paper shows no precipitation break at them at global or land-mean scale (Fig. 23f), but gives no regional or European series.
- **What it does not cover:** nothing after 2019. The streams from 2021 onwards, the dates near July 2024, any observation that started or stopped after mid-2019 and the ERA5T/final practice in 2024 are not in this paper. The ECMWF documentation (`2026 - ECMWF - ERA5 data documentation and known issues.md`) and Soci et al. (2024) are the sources for those. The check of the bridge across July 2024 against Belgian gauges is still needed.
- **ERA5T near July 2024:** the paper's statement that preliminary and final data rarely differ means that, for the bridge, the final ERA5 (and ERA5-Land) should be used wherever it exists, and any remaining preliminary months should be marked.

## Limits

- **Period:** the paper ends in 2019 (some figures in 2018), so it says nothing about 2020–2026 or July 2024.
- **Scale:** precipitation is evaluated globally, for land and sea totals, and over 50° S–50° N against TRMM; there is no European or Belgian series, and the continental comparisons are only in the supplement (Fig. S5, not read).
- **Approximate dates:** the start and end of many instruments come from the bar charts of Figs 4–6, read to within a few months; only the dates written in the text and in Table 4 are exact. Table 4 lists the period covered by a dataset, which can differ from the bars of actual use (for example CrIS 2012 in Table 4, about 2015 in Fig. 5).
- **Accumulation rule:** the paper does not define how ERA5 precipitation is accumulated (06/18 UTC forecasts, steps 1–12); that comes from the ECMWF documentation and Muñoz-Sabater et al. (2021).
- **ERA5, not ERA5-Land:** ERA5-Land is mentioned in one paragraph only; its precipitation is not discussed.
