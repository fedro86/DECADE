# CERRA, the Copernicus European Regional Reanalysis system

- **Authors, year, venue:** M. Ridal, E. Bazile, P. Le Moigne, R. Randriamampianina, S. Schimanke and others, 2024; Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society 150, 3385–3411.
- **Link:** <https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.4764>
- **Access:** open, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0
- **Read:** full text; sections 3 (observations) and 4.5 (precipitation) in detail

## What it is

The reference paper of CERRA: a 5.5 km regional reanalysis of Europe with the
HARMONIE-ALADIN system and 3D-Var, 9/1984 → 6/2021, with ERA5 at the
boundaries, an ensemble (CERRA-EDA) for the background errors, and the
land-surface reanalysis CERRA-Land, whose daily precipitation is a MESCAN
analysis of CERRA forecasts and rain gauges.

## Numbers we use

- **Precipitation against independent gauges, France 1990–2000, about 2,000
  stations a day** (section 4.5, figure 20): CERRA is better than ERA5 for all
  intensities, and CERRA-Land is the best of the four datasets compared;
  daily RMSE of CERRA 4.09 mm against 4.32 mm for ERA5.
- **Observations of the atmospheric analysis over time** (section 3.1,
  figure 3): radiosondes increase until about 2010 and then stay level or
  fall a little; aircraft reports drop steeply in 2020 (Covid-19). For the
  surface, national data are added to those of ECMWF's MARS archive, and
  Météo-France climate stations, including automatic ones.
- **Climate signal** (section 4.1): temperature trends 1985–2021 at four
  stations agree among CERRA, ERA5 and observations; nothing equivalent for
  precipitation.

precipitation is the better one; it gives no test of the homogeneity of the
precipitation analysis and no figure on gauges in Belgium. The CERRA forecast,
