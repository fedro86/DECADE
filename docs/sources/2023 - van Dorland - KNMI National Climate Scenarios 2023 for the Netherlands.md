## What it is

The scientific background report of the Dutch national climate scenarios KNMI'23, built from CMIP6 global models downscaled with EC-Earth3 and RACMO, with 1991–2020 as reference period. Section 3.2.2 puts the scenarios for seasonal mean precipitation over the Netherlands next to the observed change since the early 1900s, shown as historical 30-year normal periods (Fig. 3.10).

## Numbers we use

- **Observed change, Netherlands (section 3.2.2, p. 118):** between the early 1900s and the present day, mean precipitation increased by roughly 30 % in winter, 20 % in autumn, 10 % in summer and 5–10 % in spring; the annual mean increased by almost 20 %. The text gives no rate per decade and no test; the change is between 30-year normals, the last one being the 1991–2020 reference period.
- **Significance (p. 118–119):** the long-term summer increase (10 %) is described as "probably not significant", i.e. not statistically different from zero.
- **Recent years (p. 119):** the national summer series on the KNMI climate dashboard may show "a first indication" that the increase in summer precipitation slows down after 2000, but the authors call it too early for a conclusion.
- **Scenarios (p. 118–119):** winter increases continue the historical trend; in summer three of the four main scenarios project a decrease already by "2050" (the 30-year climate centred on 2050), opposite to the observed long-term increase; for the Netherlands plus the Rhine and Meuse basins the summer trend in the models turns negative around 2040 at the latest.

## Relevance to DECADE

H3 (#20): a level change around July 2024 as climate?

- **No published trend for the recent period.** The observed change is given only between 30-year normals ending in 2020; there is no trend for 2012–2026 or any period ending after 2020. For summer, the season of the radar overlap (1–5 July 2024), the long-term change is about 10 % in a century and probably not significant, and it may be slowing after 2000.
- **Order of magnitude.** An annual increase of almost 20 % spread over roughly a century is about 2 % per decade if linear (computed here). A step of this size cannot appear between two consecutive years; any level change at July 2024 larger than a few percent must come from year-to-year variability (documented for Belgium in the RMI overviews) or from the composites.
- **Consistent with Belgium.** The Dutch picture (winter wetter, summer not significantly changed) matches the RMI analysis for Uccle (Klimaatrapport 2020: no significant annual or summer trend since 1981).

## Limits

- **Netherlands only:** national average, no number for Belgium, Germany or France; the report does not say in this section which gauge series or homogenisation it uses.
- **Long-term, coarse:** changes between 30-year normals from the early 1900s to 1991–2020; nothing on 2021–2026, the years around the radar change.
- **Scenario report:** the observed numbers are background to the projections, not a dedicated trend study; no uncertainty range is given.
- **Only one section read:** other parts of the report (extremes, the P13 station set, hourly AWS statistics) may hold more observed numbers and were not checked.
